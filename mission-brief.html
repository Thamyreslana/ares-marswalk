02 / 07 — THE PROBLEM

The straight line is not the safe line.

From the habitat, the direct line to Rochette climbs a hill about 40 m high, with mapped slopes up to 23.2°. On foot, in a suit, carrying tools, that is the hazard to avoid. And a walk that fits at the expected pace can still lose its reserve if the return is slower.

ARES supports planning before an EVA: find a route around the terrain, then turn the time budget into one rule the crew can follow: when to leave the target.

Primary user: a crew member planning a surface science walk from a hypothetical habitat. The prototype does not provide real-time navigation or certify operational safety.

04 / 07 — DATA → METHOD → DECISION

Terrain participates in the calculation.

  • USGS/NASA CTX elevation model: 20 m source pixels, resampled to a 40 m planning grid; derive slope, exclude unknown terrain, weight the route.
  • CTX optical imagery: inspect the local surface context along the route.
  • Perseverance surface images: ground truth of what the workspace looks like up close.
  • THEMIS night infrared: where the ground is likely loose (cool at night) or rocky (warm), shown along the route; advisory, not part of the route cost.
  • Curiosity RAD: measured surface radiation dose (~0.67 mSv/day), so one EVA adds ~0.08 mSv; the acute hazard is a solar storm, answered by the walkback rule.
Route cost = distance × [1 + 4 × (slope / limit)²]
Default assumed slope limit: 12°. Unknown cells: excluded.
A* searches the numerical grid. The cost weight and slope limit are prototype assumptions, not NASA EVA policy. Small obstacles below orbital resolution remain unresolved.

06 / 07 — KNOW WHEN TO TURN BACK

One rule instead of a table of minutes.

DEFAULT EVA ASSUMPTIONS

Available time: 270 min
Walking pace: 2.5 km/h
Science work: 30 min
Required reserve: 45 min

Walkback rule: walk out at the planned pace; plan the return as if the pace drops 25%. Inspired by the walkback limits used to plan Apollo lunar rover traverses.
TERRAIN-AWARE ROUTELeave Rochette by T+135 minAllows up to 67 min of science at the target.

If fieldwork stretches to 90 min, the base case still fits, but only 67 of those minutes fit under the walkback rule. ARES tells the crew to stop and head back at T+135.

If the target lies beyond the walkback limit, the map marks where to turn back. Scenarios are explicit assumptions, not predictions of suit oxygen, fatigue, or astronaut performance.
07 / 07 — EVIDENCE, LIMITS, AND NEXT VALIDATION

A focused prototype.
An inspectable chain of evidence.

  • An EVA playback walks the plan minute by minute and raises “turn back now” when the leave-by time passes.
  • Export includes the round-trip geometry, the walkback deadline, source references, terrain policy, and all three time scenarios.
  • Automated tests cover slope-dependent routing, the blocked direct line, unknown terrain, corner cutting, walkback timing, reserve sensitivity, and map coverage.
  • Human usability validation and independent image/DEM alignment verification remain open. No such results are claimed.
