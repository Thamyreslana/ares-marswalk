02 / 07 — THE DECISION

Reach a science target.
Preserve time to return.

A short line on a map can cross steeper terrain. A route that fits at the expected pace can also lose its reserve if walking or fieldwork takes longer.

ARES supports planning before an EVA: compare routes, inspect the evidence, and test the time assumptions before committing to a sortie.

Primary user: a crew member planning a surface science walk from a hypothetical habitat. The prototype does not provide real-time navigation or certify operational safety.

03 / 07 — A TRACEABLE SCIENCE TARGET

Rochette: what can altered basalt
tell us about water?

Rochette is associated with Montdenier and Montagnac, Perseverance’s first collected rock cores. The proposed human task is to document textures and mineral context relevant to alteration by water.

04 / 07 — DATA → METHOD → DECISION

Terrain participates in the calculation.

  • CTX optical imagery: inspect the local surface context.
  • USGS/NASA CTX elevation model: 20 m source pixels, resampled to a 40 m planning grid; derive slope and exclude unknown terrain.
  • THEMIS infrared and MOLA relief: regional context, with their distinct resolutions and limitations stated.
Route cost = distance × [1 + 4 × (slope / limit)²]
Default assumed slope limit: 12°. Unknown cells: excluded.
A* searches the numerical grid. The cost weight and slope limit are prototype assumptions, not NASA EVA policy. Small obstacles below orbital resolution remain unresolved.

07 / 07 — EVIDENCE, LIMITS, AND NEXT VALIDATION

A focused prototype.
An inspectable chain of evidence.

  • Export includes the round-trip geometry, source references, terrain policy, and all three time scenarios.
  • Automated tests cover slope-dependent routing, unknown terrain, corner cutting, return time, reserve sensitivity, and map coverage.
  • Human usability validation and independent image/DEM alignment verification remain open. No such results are claimed.
