Method details

Relative cost = distance × [1 + 4 × (slope / limit)²]. A prototype weighting, not metabolic energy.

RADIATION & SPACE WEATHER

Cosmic rays are slow. Solar storms are fast.

—Cosmic-ray dose for this EVA
—Longest walk to shelter on this route, at a 25% slower pace

Curiosity’s RAD instrument measured about 0.67 mSv per day from galactic cosmic rays on the Martian surface, versus about 1.8 mSv per day during the cruise to Mars. A single EVA adds little. The acute hazard is a solar particle event, which RAD also recorded: dose rates can rise within hours, and the answer is to get back under shielding. The walkback rule keeps the crew within that reach.

Measured at Gale crater (−4.4 km). Jezero’s floor is about 1.8 km higher, under slightly less atmosphere, so the dose there is probably a little higher. Indicative, not a dosimetry model. Hassler et al. (2014), Science 343 ↗ · SwRI summary of the RAD values ↗

LIMITS

Status labels check the assumed slope limit and time reserve in the displayed scenarios; they are not a safety clearance. ARES does not model oxygen, suit performance, fatigue, current weather or obstacles smaller than the orbital data. The habitat location, pace, reserve and slope limit are planning assumptions.

THE SCIENCE QUESTION

What can altered basalt tell us about water in Jezero?

Rochette is a basaltic rock workspace associated with the Montdenier and Montagnac samples. NASA describes salts that may record interaction with water. The proposed EVA would document textures and mineral context; it does not claim evidence of life.

NASA waypoint 7_0, sol 180. This is a rover workspace position, not the exact center of a drill hole. Decimal precision is not a statement of geodetic accuracy.
