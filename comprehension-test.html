Can someone explain the decision?

A five-minute, facilitator-led comprehension test. Invite someone outside the project and observe before helping. Repeat with up to three people if time allows.

This page is a test protocol, not evidence that a user study has taken place. Observations are entered by the facilitator and are not independently verified.

Before the test

  1. Open ARES in a separate tab. Start with 270 min available, 2.5 km/h pace, 30 min science work, 45 min reserve, and 12° slope limit.
  2. Do not start the guided demo before the tasks. Avoid explaining which route the person should choose.
  3. Read one task at a time. Record whether the person completes it unaided, with help, or not at all.
Open ARES in another tab ↗

Use a code, not a name or email. Nothing is uploaded. Export saves a file on your device; unfinished notes are lost if you close the page.

01 / 03

Compare the two routes

Ask the person to identify the science destination, why the direct line is not acceptable, and how much longer the terrain-aware route is.

02 / 03

Stretch the fieldwork

Ask the person to change science work to 90 min, keeping the other settings. By what time must the crew leave Rochette, and how much of the 90 min fits?

03 / 03

Interpret the result

Ask what the green result establishes, what the app does not validate, and one reason the person might reconsider a plan.

Turn observations into the next revision

Base the revision on what you observed. A completed task is not proof of operational safety.

Facilitator reference — open after the tasks
  • Rochette is the target. The direct line crosses 10 cells above the 12° limit (up to 23.2°); the terrain-aware route adds about 178 m round trip (+3.3%).
  • With 90 min of science, the crew must leave Rochette by T+135 min; only about 67 of the 90 min fit. The base case still fits, but the conservative scenario does not.
  • Green means the configured reserve holds under the displayed assumptions. It does not certify operational safety or validate oxygen, radiation, weather, or unresolved obstacles.

Use the difficulties you observe to revise wording or layout. Record what happened, including failures; do not infer a safety benefit from this small comprehension test.
